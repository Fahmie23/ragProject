Before adopting delayed verification, the reporting institution must have appropriate risk management policies and procedures to effectively manage any ML/TF/PF risk arising from delayed verification. The delay must be essential so as not to interrupt the reporting institution’s normal conduct of business. The ML/TF/PF risks must be effectively managed during the period of delayed verification. There must be no suspicion of ML/TF/PF when delayed verification is applied. The institution’s policies and procedures must establish a reasonable timeframe for completing identity verification, which shall not exceed 10 working days or any other period specified by the supervisory authority. The policies must include measures to manage risks of delayed verification, including conditions under which the customer may use the business relationship prior to verification and appropriate limits on the number, types, or amount of transactions. The policies must require monitoring of large and complex transactions that are outside expected norms while verification is pending. Senior management must be periodically informed of any cases pending identity verification. If verification cannot be completed within the reasonable timeframe, the reporting institution should terminate the business relationship as soon as reasonably practicable and refrain from carrying out further transactions. The reporting institution must assess whether failure to complete verification within the reasonable timeframe provides sufficient grounds for filing a suspicious transaction report (STR) to the Financial Intelligence and Enforcement Department (FIED).

A reporting institution must establish appropriate measures for identification and verification of a customer’s identity before establishing a non‑face‑to‑face business relationship. For identification and verification in a non‑face‑to‑face business relationship, the institution must undertake one or more of the following: (i) request additional identification documents or information such as bank statements or utility bills; (ii) substantiate the customer’s information with an independent source, e.g., contacting the employer or verifying through a relevant authority database; (iii) contact the customer via a digital communication channel to visually identify and verify the identity; (iv) request the customer to make a nominal payment from his own account with a licensed bank to confirm true identity; (v) use new technology solutions including biometric technologies linked incontrovertibly to the customer. If the reporting institution is unable to identify and verify the customer’s identity using the measures in paragraph (c), it must initiate a face‑to‑face business relationship. (b) A reporting institution must establish appropriate measures for identification

(c) For the purpose of identification and verification of the identity of a customer in

(ii) Substantiating the customer’s information with any independent source

visually identify and verify the customer’s identity;

identity; or

technologies (e.g. fingerprint or iris scans, facial recognition), which
should be linked incontrovertibly to the customer.
